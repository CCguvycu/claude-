section 03 — project

HMOV

The other side of the work. More systems, more care, less room to be sloppy — the kind of thing where “it works on my machine” isn't an answer.

I like this one for a slightly different reason: it has to hold up. That constraint makes you think properly about how things fail, who they fail for, and what happens next. It has a purpose, so getting it right matters.

section 05 — workflow

My Coworker Is
An AI Agent.

It never asks about my weekend and it never gets tired, which is genuinely useful. It also confidently does the wrong thing sometimes, which is why I'm still here.

the development loop

↻ back to ME — with a better idea than last time

agent — session

An agent is a tool in the workflow, not a replacement for knowing what you're doing. It writes fast; I still decide what should exist, whether the tests actually prove anything, and which of its confident suggestions to throw away.

The interesting part is that it moves the work up a level. Less typing, more thinking about what to build — which is the bit I liked in the first place.

section 08 — not a fan club

Do I Question
Decisions? Obviously.

“Do I sometimes look at a decision and think, ‘What the fuck are we doing?’ Absolutely.”

“That doesn't change the fact that I love the work.”

what disagreeing looks like

Saying so. Explaining why. Sometimes being wrong about it and finding out later that the original call was better than mine.

what it doesn't look like

Quietly resenting it, or pretending every decision is brilliant. Liking your job and agreeing with everything aren't the same thing.

section 09 — the point

Most People Want To Escape Work.

I Want To Keep Building.

“Coding was already something I enjoyed before it became work. The difference now is that I get to build things that matter to other people too.”

That's the whole thing. I'm not chasing a title and I'm not counting down to a weekend. The work is the same thing I'd be doing in my own time, except now it has a reason to exist outside my own machine, and someone else gets something out of it.

I know that's not normal, and I know it might not last forever. Right now it's true, and I'm fucking grateful for it.
